Page 1

Why states need this now

Psychedelic policy is moving state by state, and every program is short on long-term, real-world data.

The federal pull

⚖︎

Executive Order 14401 · Apr 18, 2026

Directs HHS to put at least $50M into partnering with states, including data sharing, and to expand real-world evidence on psychedelics.

☰

FDA public hearing · Sep 14, 2026

One of its four topics was data collection and standardization. Speakers asked for real-world data, not just trials.

What states collect today

⚠︎

Oregon: the session day only

Client forms and quarterly center reports; no long-term outcomes. 5,935 clients in 2025.

◐

Colorado: database unfunded

SB25-297 allows a de-identified outcome database, but only if gifts or grants pay for it.

✚

New Mexico: scope not set

The law requires data rules and a de-identified annual report. The draft rules don't say what gets measured.

◉

Macrodose programs, short follow-up

Oregon, Colorado and New Mexico all center on supervised macrodose sessions. State data stops at or near the session day; the weeks of integration after it go unmeasured.

◒

Everyone else: pilots and task forces

Small clinical pilots (NJ, CT, UT), research funds (TX, AZ), reports due (MD Oct 2026, MN Jan 2027).

The closest thing to Equilibrium

◎

OHSU OPEN (Oregon)

NIDA-funded, 5 years, 1,600+ people, a year of surveys, with a comparison group. Licensed-clinic clients in one state.

✓

Where Equilibrium adds

Microdosing and daily practice, not only clinic sessions. Many states. Years, not one. Each person against their own start.

Takeaway

Federal money and federal asks now point at real-world data from states.

No state tracks microdosing, daily practice, or outcomes past a few months. That is the gap Equilibrium is built for.

New Mexico · priority 1

The medical program launches while the state decides what to measure. Microdosing is the next question, and no one has the data.

Where the program stands

1

April 2025 · Medical Psilocybin Act signed

SB 219: treatment-resistant depression, PTSD, substance use disorders, end-of-life care.

2

Aug 2026 · Producer permits open

The first permit went to Sacred Alto Spores. Clinician permits come later.

3

Oct 2, 2026 · Rules hearing

The draft rules aren't final. They add data duties but don't say what's measured.

4

Dec 2026 · Target for first patients

A year ahead of the legal deadline (Dec 31, 2027).

Why New Mexico first

◉

Supervised macrodose sessions first

The medical program starts with supervised psilocybin treatment sessions. Equilibrium follows what happens next: the integration weeks and months the program won't see.

◷

The rules are being written now

A ready model for de-identified, long-term data arrives at the right moment.

$

Money is aimed at research

A research fund for NM universities and providers, plus $300K for UNM end-of-life research and $630K for treatment equity in the FY27 budget.

☍

A community already engaged

Leaders of the NM / Santa Fe Psychedelic Society have offered a review and help reaching their community. The state's advocates and UNM's psychedelic research group both sit on the program's advisory board.

◐

Microdosing is the next question

Advocates want to move on microdosing, and the state will need data first.

Cautions

⚠︎

Privacy pushback on the draft

Critics warn the patient registry builds a sensitive state mental-health database. Equilibrium's research copy carries no names: a clear contrast.

✚

Medical only, no local decrim

Home microdosing is illegal statewide, and no New Mexico city has deprioritized it.

Takeaway

New Mexico is deciding what to measure right now, has research money, and has a community already engaged with us.

Equilibrium can arrive as the privacy-first, long-term model, and as the microdosing evidence the next debate will need.

What current research misses

Six gaps, each with the evidence behind it.

◒

1 · Non-responders

Studies report averages. Little is known about why many people don't respond.

Largest psilocybin trial for treatment-resistant depression (233 people): 37% responded at week 3 on 25 mg.

✢

2 · Combined practices

Almost no data on which non-drug practices pair with a medicine.

The FDA told Lykos to add a group without psychotherapy to separate the drug from the therapy. Integration models "cannot be described as evidence based."

◷

3 · Long-term, real-world follow-up

Trials stop at weeks. States track days.

The MDMA trials didn't test benefits past 18 weeks. Oregon tracks safety for 3 days; OHSU found new signals at 1 and 3 months.

◔

4 · Microdosing at scale, with controls

Big studies lack controls. Controlled studies are small and short.

Largest observational study: 953 microdosers over 30 days, not built to measure expectancy. Self-blinding study: 191 people, placebo did about as well. A 44-study review calls the "it's all placebo" verdict premature.

≠

5 · Shared definitions

No agreed definition of a microdose, or of integration.

The 44-study review proposed standard dose ranges. Integration researchers call for a consensus definition.

◑

6 · Who gets studied

Samples skew white and college educated.

OHSU's Oregon cohort: 86.7% white, 76.6% college educated.

Takeaway

All six gaps share one blind spot: the field studies one medicine at a time, for weeks, on average.

The combination lock sits right inside that blind spot.
